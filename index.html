📍 Location Guide

Know Your Market

Three distinct communities on the DR's North Coast — each with its own character, price point, and investment profile.

Sosua, Dominican Republic

Known for its stunning horseshoe bay, clear waters ideal for snorkeling, and vibrant expat community, Sosua is the North Coast's most established real estate market. El Batey (the main town center) offers walkable access to restaurants, the beach, and services — making it a top pick for short-term rental investors and retirees alike.

Gated communities including Ocean Village, Sosua Hills, and Casa Linda offer resort-style amenities. The town is 15 minutes from Luperón Airport (POP) with direct North American and European flights.

Sosua Bay

One of the Caribbean's most beautiful horseshoe beaches. World-class snorkeling, diving, and a charming coastal town with a strong international community.

Cabarete, Dominican Republic

The adventure sports capital of the Caribbean — world-famous for kitesurfing, windsurfing, and surfing at Encuentro Beach. Cabarete attracts a younger, energetic crowd of digital nomads, water sports enthusiasts, and boutique resort developers. The restaurant and nightlife scene is among the best on the island.

Beachfront condos start at $150K with strong short-term rental demand. New projects continue to break ground, with pre-construction prices offering excellent entry-level value.

Puerto Plata, Dominican Republic

The province capital offers a broader range of property types — from affordable inland homes to oceanview condos near Cofresi Beach. Puerto Plata is the gateway to the entire North Coast with Luperón Airport, major hospital infrastructure, international schools, and a growing boutique hotel scene fueled by the city's colonial heritage.

Properties here offer excellent value, with appreciation accelerating as airport upgrades and road expansion projects continue through 2026–2027.

❓ FAQ

Common Questions About Sosua Real Estate

Everything you need to know before investing in Dominican Republic property.

The Dominican Republic imposes no restrictions on foreign property ownership. Foreigners enjoy identical rights to Dominican nationals when purchasing real estate in Sosua, Cabarete, or Puerto Plata. A deslinde (title survey) and notario are the standard tools for a clean transfer.
Law 158-01 (CONFOTUR) is the Dominican Republic's tourism investment incentive program. Properties approved under this law receive up to 15 years of exemption from the annual IPI property tax (normally 1%) and from the 3% property transfer tax. Buying a CONFOTUR-approved property is considered one of the smartest financial decisions available to investors in the DR market.
Short-term rental yields in Sosua range from 7–12% annually. Beachfront properties and well-positioned condos in gated communities achieve the higher end of this range. Sosua has over 1,500 active Airbnb listings with median monthly earnings around $1,322 USD and peak season occupancy rates of 70–80%.
Property values in Sosua have doubled over the past 5–10 years, with the sharpest appreciation from 2020–2025 driven by remote work trends and rising international demand. Beachfront properties have tripled in some cases. Gated community homes appreciated 8–12% annually, while standard residential properties saw 5–8% per year during this period.
Standard purchases incur a 3% property transfer tax and an annual IPI property tax of 1% on value above approximately $177,000 USD. Properties below this threshold owe zero annual property tax. CONFOTUR-approved properties are exempt from both taxes for up to 15 years. There is no capital gains tax in the Dominican Republic for individual property sales.
Local Dominican banks offer mortgages to foreigners, typically requiring 30–40% down payment. Developer financing is widely available for pre-construction projects, often with staged payment plans. Many North American and European buyers use home equity or portfolio financing from their home country, then purchase in cash — giving negotiation leverage in the DR market.

Working with a reputable real estate attorney (not just an agent) is strongly recommended for any foreign buyer.
